Our story

We are kirra services

Kirra Services is a Canberra-based, majority Indigenous owned and Supply Nation Certified information technology provider, established in 2017.

Our founder and managing director, Bradley Nagle, is a proud Bundjalung man with cultural connections back to Fingal Head (the Phillips family), a small township of Tweed Heads on the far north coast of NSW. His mother was raised in Murwillumbah on the Tweed River, where his grandfather was born.

Kirra, the namesake of our business, is a beautiful beach in Queensland where Brad's grandfather spent time throughout his life. Kirra also means "leaf" in the local language, and it is an important part of our connection to the land and to Country. It's why a leaf sits at the heart of our logo.

Family, community and the Top End

Brad and his family spent much of 2018 living in Arnhem Land in the Northern Territory, then returned to the Aboriginal community of Gapuwiyak in East Arnhem Land for 2020 and the first half of 2021, where Brad's wife taught at the local school. Together they work to improve the future opportunities of First Nations people, and that experience shapes how we run the business every day.

The mission

Brad's mission for Kirra Services has never changed: build an inclusive, high-quality, great-value ICT business that offers more opportunities for Indigenous participation in the ICT industry. Today that means six offices, Tier 1 vendor partnerships, a recruitment team placing people across government and enterprise, and a training arm, Kirra Academy, that turns learning into real jobs.

Connection to Country

Respect, first and always

Kirra Services acknowledges all Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Traditional Custodians of the lands on which we work and live. We pay our respect to their Elders – past and present and recognise their continuing connection to land, sea, culture and community.

We also recognise the important role Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people continue to play within the greater Canberra community and across every region we work in. That acknowledgement isn't a line in a footer for us. It's the reason the business exists, and it guides who we hire, who we partner with and where our profit goes.

Kirra on Country

In 2026, Brad and Trish drove more than 4,000 kilometres from Canberra to Darwin on the Red Dirt Road Mission, travelling through Country, delivering resources, listening and connecting with communities along the way. The first delivery included sports balls, uniforms and ten laptops for digital technology classes. Showing up in person and building relationships is how we believe this work should be done.

For government buyers

Indigenous Procurement Policy

Procuring from an Indigenous-owned business is the easiest and fastest way for federal government departments and entities to buy goods or services.

The Australian Government's Indigenous Procurement Policy (IPP) is a mandatory procurement-connected policy under the Commonwealth Procurement Rules, administered by the National Indigenous Australians Agency (NIAA). It uses the Commonwealth's multi-billion-dollar annual spend to drive demand for Indigenous goods and services, stimulate Indigenous economic development and grow the Indigenous business sector.

How the IPP works

  • Annual targets for the volume and value of contracts awarded to Indigenous businesses by the Commonwealth and each portfolio.
  • Mandatory Set Aside (MSA): Indigenous businesses get the chance to demonstrate value for money before a general approach to market, for procurements delivered in remote Australia, and for all other procurements wholly delivered in Australia valued between $80,000 and $200,000 (GST inclusive).
  • Mandatory Minimum Indigenous Participation Requirements (MMR) apply to contracts wholly delivered in Australia valued at $7.5 million or more in 19 industries.
  • Exemption 16 lets portfolios procure directly from Indigenous small and medium enterprises (fewer than 200 full-time equivalent staff) at any value, provided value for money is demonstrated and the right level of sign-off is in place.

Why it suits ICT buying

Exemption 16 is a great solution for the urgent supply of products or services, or when you are managing multiple procurements, and it lets you procure items above your department's usual threshold. Kirra holds the highest Supply Nation accreditation, at the certified level, with more than 50 per cent Indigenous ownership by our managing director, who works actively in the business. Each department may have its own procurement rules, so talk to us about how an engagement is reported against your agency's targets.

Ask a question
Who owns Kirra Services?

Kirra Services is majority Indigenous owned by its founder and managing director, Bradley Nagle, a proud Bundjalung man who works actively in the business. We are Supply Nation Certified and NSWICC Assured.

What does "Kirra" mean?

Kirra means "leaf" in the local language, and it is an important part of our connection to the land and Country. Kirra is also a beach in Queensland where Brad's grandfather spent time throughout his life.

Can government agencies buy from Kirra without going to market?

Often, yes. Exemption 16 of the Commonwealth Procurement Rules allows portfolios to procure directly from Indigenous small and medium enterprises at any value, provided value for money is demonstrated. The Mandatory Set Aside also gives Indigenous businesses the first opportunity on procurements between $80,000 and $200,000 (GST inclusive). Check your department's own rules too.

Do I need one quote or three under the IPP?

The policy does not specify a required number of quotes. It requires that you demonstrate value for money, and to establish that you may choose to seek a number of quotes.

Which panels is Kirra on?

Federal panels include the DTA ICT Panels and Digital Marketplace, the ATO Contingent Labour Hire Panel (Indigenous Businesses), Procurement Australia, the GovCMS Drupal Services Panel and the Department of Finance People Panel. We are also on state, territory and local government arrangements in NSW, Victoria, Queensland, the NT and WA.

How does Kirra give back to community?

We are a profit-for-purpose business. Profit funds pathway programs such as the Cyber Career Starter Program and Kirra Academy, community projects locally and interstate, and Kirra on Country deliveries of technology and sporting gear to remote communities.
